Are you interested in uncovering the physical properties that govern life inside a cell?
This master’s project offers the opportunity to investigate the cytoplasmic viscosity of cancer cells in vivo and how it is regulated by the cell’s physical environment.<br>
Cytoplasmic viscosity is a key biophysical parameter that controls intracellular transport and sets fundamental limits on the speed of biochemical reactions through diffusion. Despite its central role in virtually all cellular processes, it remains poorly characterized in vivo, largely due to the experimental challenges associated with measuring it inside living cells. In this project, you will use advanced microscopy techniques to quantify cytoplasmic viscosity by tracking the motion of tracer beads embedded within the cytoplasm of living cells. The work combines biochemical skills (cell culture, transfection, viral transduction), technical development (working with a home-built microscopy setup and devices to apply controlled mechanical pressure), and advanced data analysis (DDM and TICS analysis to extract spatially resolved intracellular viscosity). After mastering these techniques, you will perform a systematic study of how external physical parameters, such as substrate stiffness and applied pressure, affect cytoplasmic viscosity. By linking the mechanical environment of the cell to its internal physical state, this project aims to provide new insights into how cancer cells adapt to and sense their surroundings.<br>
