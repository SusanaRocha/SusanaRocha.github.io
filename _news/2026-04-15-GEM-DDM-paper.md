Happy to share our new paper on quantifying cytoplasmic viscosity in colorectal cancer cells.<br>
While GEMs (genetically encoded multimeric nanoparticles) have been used before to probe the viscoelastic properties of the cytosol, many previous studies relied on single-particle tracking (SPT). In practice, that often meant working with cells showing low GEM expression and using more advanced/less accessible experimental setups.<br>
In this work, we show that differential dynamic microscopy (DDM) can be used as a powerful alternative. By analyzing the collective fluctuations of GEMs, DDM provides a more robust and accessible way to extract quantitative information on cytoplasmic viscosity.<br>

A very nice team effort, and a reminder that sometimes improving how we measure biology is exactly what allows us to understand it better.<br>
