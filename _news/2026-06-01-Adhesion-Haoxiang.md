Can we control how cells migrate by changing a single biochemical cue in their environment?<br>
In our latest work, we used synthetic fibrous hydrogels to explore this question.<br>
By presenting human stem cells with either an integrin-binding ligand (RGD), a cadherin-mimicking ligand (HAVDI), or no adhesive ligand, we were able to steer cells toward mesenchymal-like, hybrid, or amoeboid-like migration behaviors. <br>

Importantly, these changes occurred without altering the bulk mechanics or architecture of the matrix.<br>
Different ligands led to distinct adhesion structures, force transmission pathways, matrix remodeling patterns, and YAP activation, revealing how biochemical cues can shape migration strategy in 3D environments.<br>
This work provides a simple and highly tunable platform to study migration plasticity and cell-matrix interactions in development, regeneration, and disease.<br>

Congratulations to **Haoxiang** for leading the project and to all co-authors for their contributions!<br>
